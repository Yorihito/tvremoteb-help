Connecting & pairing

Preparing the TV (one time only)

To control the TV from the app, enable these two things on the TV first (from the BRAVIA settings menu):

  • IP control — Settings → Network & Internet → Home network setup → IP control → set authentication to “Normal and Pre-Shared Key” or “Normal.”
  • Remote start — also under Home network setup. With this on, the app can power the TV on even from standby.
Your iPhone and TV must be on the same Wi-Fi network. Networks that isolate devices from each other (such as guest Wi-Fi) won’t work.

Connection screen (choosing a TV)

This opens on first launch, or when you tap the button at the top left of the header.

  • TV to connect — your list of saved TVs. Tap to connect; swipe left to delete.
  • TVs found on your network — BRAVIA (Google TV) sets discovered automatically on the same Wi-Fi. Tap one to register and connect.
  • Add a TV manually — if it isn’t found automatically, enter the IP address directly. The name is optional; the MAC address is used for power-on (Wake-on-LAN) but is fetched automatically after connecting, so you can usually leave it blank.
You can find the TV’s IP address under Settings → Network & Internet → Network status on the TV.

PIN pairing

Do the first pairing with the TV powered on. While the screen is off (standby) the TV can't display a PIN, so pairing can't start — turn the TV on with its remote first, then connect.
  1. Select a TV and connect; a 4-digit PIN appears on the TV screen.
  2. Enter those 4 digits in the field on your iPhone.
    While the field shows “Preparing…”, wait a moment.
  3. Tap Pair.
    On success the credentials are stored securely on your iPhone (Keychain) and it reconnects automatically next time — no PIN needed.

If pairing fails, an error is shown. If the PIN on the TV disappears, cancel and reconnect to get a fresh PIN.

Shared header (top of every screen)

WhereWhat it does
Left buttonOpens the connection screen (switch / add TVs).
CentreThe connected TV name and a state badge: On Standby Not connected.
Right buttonPower toggle. Press it while the TV is on (icon glows green) to go to standby, or while in standby to power on.

When something goes wrong a banner appears below the header. Red is a “connection error” (the TV is unreachable, with a retry button); amber is a “command error” (still connected, but that button failed). Dismiss with ✕.

Even from standby, pressing YouTube, a number key, etc. wakes the TV. The app’s state indicator follows along to “On” automatically.

Media screen (jog / shuttle)

Layout

  • Playback info card (top) — shows the current title and source when the TV/app provides playback information. Tap it to refresh. Apps that do not expose playback information show “No playback info.”
  • Mode switch — Jog (frame) and Shuttle (variable).
  • Jog dial — drag your finger around the rim to turn it.
  • Round button in the dial’s center — sends “OK / confirm.” It depresses with a click.
  • Vertical wheel (right) and horizontal wheel (below) — move the cursor up/down/left/right. Together with the dial they act like a D-pad.
  • Back button — next to the horizontal wheel.
  • Transport row (bottom) — Prev / Rewind / Play / Pause / Forward / Next.

Jog mode (small-step seeking)

Turning the dial repeatedly nudges the video forward or back a little at a time. Clockwise goes forward, counter-clockwise goes back. Great for rewinding a scene you missed by just a touch.

The rotation needed per nudge is adjustable with “Frame-step sensitivity” in Settings.

Shuttle mode (variable speed)

How far you twist the dial sets the fast-forward / rewind speed tier — the further you twist, the faster, with the center readout (e.g. ≫ ×2) showing the current speed. Release and the dial springs back to center and returns to normal playback.

The angle per tier is adjustable with “Shuttle sensitivity” in Settings.

There’s no “smooth seek” command on the TV, so Jog/Shuttle is approximated by sending a stream of remote-button presses. How fine and responsive it feels varies by TV model and the app/content (a recording vs. streaming, etc.). It works best with recorded shows.

Keypad screen

For direct channel entry and the extra utility buttons that don’t fit on the main remote.

  • Terrestrial / BS·CS / 4K — tuner switches (here too, since they pair with number entry).
  • Number keys 1–12 — direct channel selection. “10” is the 0 key; 11 and 12 include sub-channel / tune labels.
  • · / 3-digit / Enter — for entering 3-digit channel numbers.
  • Utility buttons — Data (the “d” button) / Subtitle / Audio / Display / Help / Stop / REC (record) / Recorder / Quick settings.

Apps screen

Lists the apps installed on the TV and launches one on tap. After launching, it switches to the Remote screen automatically so you can keep navigating.

  • The list is fetched from the TV automatically. Pull down to refresh.
  • For your most-used video apps, registering them as streaming buttons on the Remote screen launches them faster.

Settings screen

User guide

At the very top of Settings is a link that opens this user guide in your browser. It opens this English version when the app language is English, the Japanese version when it is Japanese, and follows the iPhone’s preferred language when set to System.

Language

Choose the app’s display language: “System (match iPhone),” “Japanese,” or “English.” Changes take effect after you restart the app (swipe it up to quit, then reopen).

Connected TV

Shows the name, IP address, and MAC address of the TV you’re connected to. The MAC address is used to power on from standby (Wake-on-LAN) and only appears when it could be fetched from the TV.

Remote streaming buttons

Choose which video-app buttons appear on the Remote screen — up to 3 from hulu / NETFLIX / U-NEXT / prime video / ABEMA / Disney+ / YouTube / My BRAVIA. Tap to toggle each on/off (when 3 are selected, remove one to free a slot).

Volume presets

This remembers a preferred volume per app or broadcast and applies it automatically when you switch — e.g. “volume 15 when YouTube opens, 30 when returning to TV.” It compensates for the fact that different apps have different baseline volumes on the TV.

Recommended steps:

  1. Turn on “Enable volume presets.”
  2. Set the TV to your usual volume, then tap the “…” menu on each row → “Remember current volume.”
    The volume currently set on the TV becomes the preset. You can also adjust the number directly with +/−.
  3. Rows left as “Not set” don’t change the volume.
    Use “…” → “Clear preset” to return a row to Not set anytime.

Fine tuning (the defaults are usually fine):

ItemMeaning
Delay before applyingHow long after launching an app before the volume starts changing. If your TV launches apps slowly and the change misses before launch completes, make this longer.
Volume step intervalThe gap between each ±1 step when changing volume. If the volume doesn’t reach the target or overshoots, a slightly longer interval is more reliable.
Delay before verifyingHow long before the app checks that the target volume was actually reached. Normally no need to change.

Tab order

Reorder the bottom tabs (Remote, Keypad, Media, Apps, Settings). Tap “Edit” at the top right of the section, then drag the rows to reorder. Putting your most-used screens near the edge keeps them within thumb reach.

Jog settings

ItemMeaning
HapticsTurns the vibration (click feel) on buttons and the dial on/off.
Frame-step sensitivityThe rotation needed to step once in Jog mode. Smaller is more sensitive (a small turn steps), larger is more deliberate.
Shuttle sensitivityThe angle needed to raise the speed one tier in Shuttle mode. Smaller means a small twist speeds up more.

Jog step accuracy varies by TV model and content. Find what feels good in your setup.

Motion controls (experimental)

  • Flip to mute — when on, setting the iPhone face-down on a surface mutes the TV, and lifting it back up unmutes. Handy for silencing the TV instantly when you take a call or step away, without hunting for the remote.

It only works while the app is open. It only undoes mutes the app itself triggered, so it never overrides a mute you set by hand. It waits a moment before reacting, so simply setting the phone down or picking it up doesn’t trip it by accident.

Switch / reset / unpair

ItemDescription
Switch to another TVOpens the connection screen. You can register and use several TVs.
Reset settings to defaultsRestores volume presets, jog settings, streaming choices, and tab order to their defaults. Your pairing (the connection to the TV) is kept, and language and developer mode are unchanged. There’s a confirmation dialog, so a stray tap is safe.
Unpair this TVDeletes the saved credentials and the TV’s registration, and resets settings to defaults. Reconnecting requires PIN pairing again. Use it when you replace the TV or want a clean start.

Developer mode

Leave this off for normal use. When on, it shows the list of remote commands (IRCC codes) your TV supports and lets you send them with a tap to test. It’s for checking “does this button work on my TV?”

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to check
The TV isn’t foundConfirm the iPhone and TV are on the same Wi-Fi. Check that “Local Network” is allowed under iPhone Settings → TV REMOTE for B. If still not found, add it by IP address manually.
Stuck on “Connecting…” / “The TV screen is off”The TV must be powered on for the first pairing. Turn it on with its remote, then tap “Connect” again.
Connection errorsConfirm “IP control” is enabled on the TV. Restarting the TV (unplug/replug) can also help.
Won’t power on from standbyEnable “Remote start” on the TV. Some Wi-Fi router settings can block the wake packet.
Some buttons don’t workRemote commands vary by TV model and firmware. A few actions (such as slow playback) aren’t supported on some models.
“Back” needs two pressesIn apps like YouTube, the first “Back” during playback only hides the on-screen controls. Press it again (that’s the TV app’s behavior).
No playback info appearsSome apps do not provide playback information. Seeing “No playback info” does not mean the app or TV connection is broken.
Volume presets don’t applyCheck the preset isn’t left as “Not set.” If the app launches slowly, increase “Delay before applying.”
